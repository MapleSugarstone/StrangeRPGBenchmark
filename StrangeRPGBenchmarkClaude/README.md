# Duotone

A tile-based adventure game in the shape of a turn-based party RPG. Every sprite is 8 by 8 pixels, generated by code, and uses at most three colors. The game runs in a small square in a web page, and all dialogue appears inside that square.

## Play

Double-click `play.cmd`. It installs the build tools on first run, builds the game, and opens `dist/index.html` in your browser. If a build fails it opens the last good build. It needs Node 18 or newer.

- Arrows or WASD move.
- Z, Space, or Enter confirms and talks.
- X or Esc cancels and opens the menu.
- C shifts between past and present once you have the Pocket Sundial.
- M mutes sound.

The title screen has **Chapter select**, which starts any chapter with a party, level, and gear set to what the playthrough bot has at that point. Saving happens at lamps, at inns, from the menu, and at every chapter start.

## The world

Tessel is a flat world of tiles printed by the Loom, a machine that hangs in the sky where a sun would be. Everything it prints gets three inks: black, plus two hues. The hero, Wick, got black and amber and nothing else. The village calls that a misprint.

The three-color rule is also the combat system. A creature's two colors are its two hues, and hues have opposites on a six-hue wheel. Read an enemy's colors and you know what hurts it.

The overarching story follows the twelve stages of the hero's journey across eight chapters. Each chapter is its own eight-step story circle. `Notes/story.md` maps every beat.

## Chapters, companions, and mechanics

Each chapter adds a battle mechanic, a field mechanic, and a new party member.

| Chapter | Journey stage | New companion | Battle mechanic | Field mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Misprint | Ordinary world, call, refusal, mentor | Nona, a nine-tailed maintenance cat | Core battle, Ninth Life revives | A dark wood lit by the lamps you light |
| 2. Hue and Cry | Crossing the threshold | Tint, a hue-witch | Hues and weaknesses, repainting foes | Paint pools and color gates |
| 3. Bell Jar | Tests, allies, enemies | Brask, armor full of moths | Shells and breaks | Bell switches |
| 4. The Second Hand | Tests, allies, enemies | Tock, a monk who lives backward | Visible turn order you can push | Shifting between past and present |
| 5. The Undermarket | Tests, allies, enemies | VEND, a vending-machine golem | Skills that cost gold | Conveyor belts, and a choice with consequences |
| 6. The Tether | Approach to the inmost cave | Mirrow, an escaped reflection | Link attacks, reflecting any action | Sailing the cloud sea |
| 7. The Loom | The ordeal | Nil, a Blank | Echoing enemy skills | Quiet rooms where hues do nothing |
| 8. The Third Ink | Road back, resurrection, return | The scattered party returns | A third hue for Wick | A grey world, and two endings |

## Balance and fun measurement

`npm run sim` runs the balance suite. It writes three files to `reports/`:

- `report.html`, a page with charts: fun index by chapter, a heatmap of every fun metric, boss win rates by player skill, the level curve, and play time.
- `balance.md`, the same data as tables, plus random-encounter results, action variety, and new content per chapter.
- `metrics.json`, the raw numbers.

The suite has three parts.

1. **Content checks** confirm that every map, warp, marker, encounter group, enemy, and skill resolves.
2. **A playthrough bot** starts a new game and plays all eight chapters along each chapter's story route. It solves the paint, time, and belt puzzles with a search over those states, opens every reachable chest, lights every lamp, fights every random encounter it walks into, heals between fights, rests at a lamp when low, and shops for weapons. It records levels, battles, losses, steps, gold, and dialogue length. If the bot cannot finish a chapter, the report names the step it failed on.
3. **Monte Carlo battles** replay each boss hundreds of times from the bot's exact state before the fight, with four simulated players: smart (reads hues, heals, guards telegraphed attacks, rations ink), casual (plays smart two thirds of the time), mash (attacks every turn), and random.

The fun index combines thirteen measurable proxies, each scored against a target band: challenge, agency, accessibility, tension, boss pacing, random-fight pacing, attrition, whether skill matters, action variety, freedom from grinding, novelty, use of the hue system, and chapter length. The report explains each band.

`node build/sim.cjs --tune` searches boss HP and damage scales for values that put both smart and casual players in their target bands, and prints suggestions.

## Development

- `npm run build` builds once. `npm run watch` rebuilds on save.
- `npm run typecheck` runs the TypeScript checker.
- `npm run sim` builds and runs the balance suite. Add `--quick` after `node build/sim.cjs` for a faster run.

Source layout:

- `src/core`: RNG, palette and hue math, pixel font, sprite generators, renderer, input, and synthesized audio.
- `src/battle`: the battle engine, which the game and the simulator share, plus enemy AI and simulated players.
- `src/game`: game state, the scripting context, the map model, and chapter presets.
- `src/scenes`: title, field, dialogue, battle, menu, shop, chapter card, game over, and ending screens.
- `src/maps`: one module per chapter with its maps, characters, and scripts.
- `src/sim`: the playthrough bot, battle simulator, fun model, and report writer.
