# The Long Noon

Polished by Opus 5.5 medium.

The Long Noon is a tile-based role-playing game that runs in a small square on a web page. Every sprite is 8 by 8 pixels, generated by code, and uses at most three colors. The story is a twelve chapter hero's journey, and each chapter is an eight step story circle. Combat is turn based and gains one new mechanic and one new partymate per chapter.

The game is written in TypeScript and has no runtime dependencies. The repository also contains a balance lab that plays the game with bots and reports numbers about difficulty, pacing, and variety.

## Play

Double-click `play.cmd`. It installs the build tools on the first run, builds the game, and opens `dist/index.html` in your default browser.

To serve the game from a local address instead, run `npm run serve` and open http://localhost:5173.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| Arrow keys or WASD | Move and choose |
| Z, Enter, or Space | Confirm and talk |
| X, Escape, or C | Cancel and open the menu |

On a touch screen the page shows an on-screen pad.

Useful addresses while you look around:

- `?ch=7` starts at chapter 7 with the party at the expected level.
- `?sheet` shows every generated sprite (left and right arrows change the page).
- The title screen has a CHAPTER SELECT entry that does the same as `?ch=`.

The game saves to the browser when you use a save crystal and at the start of each chapter.

## The story

It has been noon for three hundred years. Wick, an apprentice who winds the brass Lamp that serves as the sun, finds a forgotten setting on the Lamp dial called DUSK. A vending machine, a moth postmaster, a door shaped like a knight, a retired dragon accountant, a ghost bus, and seven others join the trip to find out who stopped the evening and why.

The setting mixes science fiction and fantasy without apology. A village lives in the cafeteria of a crashed starship. Undeliverable mail forms a golem. A casino floats in the sky and plays chess against itself forever. A fallen star is a forge. The world is a quilt of regions sewn together along seams.

The story has 6800 scripted words, 39 named speakers, and two endings chosen in the final chapter.

## Structure

Each chapter is one hero's journey stage and plays out as eight story circle beats: you, need, go, search, find, take, return, change. The map for a chapter is a ring of eight rooms, one per beat, and the last beat brings you back to the room where you started. A gate between two rooms opens when the beat before it finishes. The journal in the menu lists the eight beats for the current chapter.

## Combat

Turns come from a timeline ordered by speed. Damage scales with the attacker stat and falls with the target defense. Six elements (ember, frost, volt, lumen, umbra, bloom) have weaknesses and resistances. A party of four fights at a time from a roster of thirteen, and every partymate has six skills and a limit skill. Gear is shared by the whole party, so a purchase helps everyone.

The mechanics stack. By chapter 12 a fight uses the timeline, openings from weaknesses, nerve, stances, rows, reactions, fields, last gasps, runes, rewind, ascension, and dusk.

## Sprites

No image files exist. `src/gfx/sprites.ts` builds every sprite in code:

- Party members are hand-authored 8 by 8 grids with three palette slots.
- Monsters are mirrored silhouettes from twelve body archetypes, mutated by a seed, then outlined and speckled.
- Bosses are a 16 by 16 silhouette cut into four 8 by 8 sprites, each with at most three colors.
- Townsfolk are assembled from head and body parts chosen by a hash of their name.
- Map tiles are recolored per chapter from two theme colors and black.

## Balance and fun

The balance lab uses four bots: a smart bot that scores every legal action, a casual bot that mostly casts a damaging skill and heals when someone is low, a mash bot that only attacks, and a random bot. The report covers:

- Win rates and fight length per chapter for each bot.
- Level sensitivity: how a boss win rate changes when the party is behind or ahead of the expected level.
- A full campaign that shops, rests, and levels between chapters.
- Win rates for sampled parties, to check that each partymate can carry a slot.
- Damage, healing, and skill usage per character.
- Adoption of each new mechanic in boss fights.
- Story structure counts.

Fun is not measurable directly, so the report computes twelve proxies and combines them into one number. Each proxy has a target band: decision depth, boss tension, regular fight ease, comeback drama, pacing, smoothness across chapters, forgiveness when under-leveled, level tracking, skill variety, party viability, mechanic adoption, and story completeness. The score ranks builds against each other and does not claim to measure enjoyment.

Random encounters come every 18 to 31 steps, and a room has no more random encounters after it has had three. A won random fight restores 30% of HP and 20% of MP to the party and pays 1.8 times the base experience and gold. Running from a regular fight succeeds at least 70% of the time.

Two rules keep the game from spiraling. Foe levels never exceed the party average by more than one (regular) or one and a half (boss), and experience is worth more to a party that trails the expected level. A party that loses a fight twice in a row faces weaker foes on the next try.

## Extras

- The title screen has a BALANCE LAB entry. The game plays itself with the smart bot and charts regular and boss win rates for every chapter.
- Each chapter has a generated chiptune loop with its own scale, progression, and tempo. SOUND in the menu turns audio off.
- The vault room of every chapter holds a chest guarded by a returning boss.

## Latest results

`reports/balance-report.md` holds the full tables. The last run gave a composite proxy score of 90 out of 100. The smart bot wins about 98% of regular fights and 45 to 95% of boss fights depending on the party. Attack-only play loses every boss, so skills and supplies matter. A scripted and an autopilot playthrough both reach the ending. The weakest proxies are skill variety (each partymate has one dominant skill against a single boss) and party viability for support-heavy parties.

## Layout

```
play.cmd            builds and opens the game
src/core/           battle engine, party, bots, map generator, script runner
src/data/           skills, characters, items, enemies, tuning
src/story/          twelve chapters of scripted beats
src/gfx/            font, screen buffer, sprite generators
src/game/           scenes: title, world, battle, menus, ending, music
src/sim/            simulation helpers shared by the tools
src/tools/          validation, playthrough, balance, and tuning programs
reports/            generated balance report
```
